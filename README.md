# Policy-documentation
<p>As the Cybersecurity Analyst assigned to the Healthcare Clinical & Administrative Centre, I was
tasked with developing a complete, usable suite of information security policies to protect
Protected Health Information (PHI) and Electronic PHI (ePHI) in compliance with HIPAA and
HITECH. Rather than writing policies from scratch, which risks missing established best
practices, produces inconsistent formatting, and creates documents that do not align to
recognised frameworks, I used two industry-standard policy template sources as my foundation:
the ISO 27001:2022 Annex A control framework and the SANS Institute free policy template
library.</p>
 </p>Starting from established templates served three critical purposes. First, it ensured structural
completeness, ISO 27001 and SANS templates have been developed and refined by
information security professionals over decades, and using them as a starting point means I
begin with a policy that already contains all the sections a good policy needs. Second, it
provided immediate audit alignment, any auditor examining our policies will recognise the ISO
27001 structure and SANS format, which increases the credibility and defensibility of the policy
suite. Third, it saved time without sacrificing rigour, customising a proven template for the
healthcare and HIPAA context is faster and more reliable than drafting from a blank page.
The result of this process is six complete, signed-off, usable security policies, each one
traceable to its template source, mapped to specific ISO 27001 Annex A controls, and tailored to
the organisation's VLAN architecture, HIPAA obligations, and specific risk register findings.
Every policy in this suite can be handed directly to staff, posted to the intranet, and presented to
an ISO 27001 or HIPAA auditor without further modification.</p>
